How It Works

From exception to resolved brief
in under 5 minutes

OpsLens handles the entire detection-to-brief pipeline automatically, with zero manual steps.

1
T + 0 min

Exception fires in your infrastructure

OpsLens monitors Docker container logs and log files. An ERROR, EXCEPTION, or CRITICAL line appears in your checkout-worker container.

2
T + 2 min

Immediate Slack alert — no LLM delay

The 5-minute fast scanner detects the spike, checks known issue suppression, resolves team routing rules, and fires a raw Slack alert to the Payments Team channel — before any LLM is invoked.

3
T + 3 min

RAG enrichment from all connected sources

The exception is embedded and matched against your Qdrant vector store. Related Jira tickets, Slack threads, and GitHub issues surface automatically. An AI diagnosis follows in a second Slack message.

4
T + 4 min

Delivery Risk Timeline query — what changed?

OpsLens queries the last 60 minutes of your Delivery Risk Timeline: PRs merged, deploys triggered, Jira status transitions. It finds that PR #87 merged to main 35 minutes before the spike.

5
T + 4.5 min

Full RRT Brief delivered to Slack

A structured incident artifact lands in the channel: AI-written title, what happened, business impact, suspected root cause (citing PR #87), related context grouped by source, and numbered next actions. Stored in DB with open status.

6
T + N min

Engineer resolves and closes the brief

One API call (or a Slack update) flips the brief to investigating, then resolved. Resolution notes are stored. The team gets a Slack notification. Incident history is preserved for post-mortems.

OpsLens uses webhooks (GitHub, Jira), OAuth integrations for Slack, and direct API connections for your vector data via Qdrant. For Docker log scanning, the worker runs alongside your containers and pulls logs directly — no agents or sidecars required. Setup typically takes under 30 minutes.

It depends on your LLM provider choice. With OpenAI or Claude, exception snippets and related context are sent to their API. With Ollama (self-hosted LLM), all data stays within your network. Enterprise customers can also deploy OpsLens fully on-prem with their own Qdrant and Ollama instances.

An RRT (Rapid Response Team) Brief is a structured incident artifact generated automatically when a critical exception is detected. It includes an AI-written title, what happened, business impact, suspected root cause (referencing recent PRs and deploys), related Jira/GitHub/Slack context, and numbered next actions. It's delivered to each matched team's Slack channel and stored in the database with a full lifecycle (open → investigating → resolved).

PagerDuty and OpsGenie are excellent at alerting and on-call scheduling. OpsLens is an intelligence layer built on top — it enriches alerts with context from your existing tools, auto-generates structured briefs, and correlates incidents with code changes. Think of OpsLens as the AI co-pilot that tells you why an alert fired and what changed before it, rather than just who to page. PagerDuty integration (routing key) is already in the roadmap.

You create a rule via the API or dashboard that matches an exception by signature hash (exact match on the normalised exception line), regex/keyword pattern, or Jira ticket key. Rules can be permanent, time-boxed (snooze until a date), or auto-expire when the linked Jira ticket moves to Done. Hit counts are tracked so you can see how many times a suppressed issue has fired.
